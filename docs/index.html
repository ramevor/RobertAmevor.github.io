PhD Student in Biostatistics

I am Robert Amevor, a PhD student in Biostatistics at the University of South Carolina’s Arnold School of Public Health.

My research focuses on adaptive clinical trial design, Bayesian inference, survival analysis, Phase I dose-finding, and statistical genomics, with applications in cancer and neurodegenerative disease.

I am particularly interested in developing statistical methods that improve the design, safety, and efficiency of clinical research while supporting reproducible and data-driven biomedical science.

Research Focus

Adaptive Clinical Trials

Developing statistical methodology for Phase I clinical trials, including adaptive extensions of the Time-to-Event Continual Reassessment Method (TITE-CRM).

Statistical Genomics

Using RNA-seq and longitudinal biomedical data to identify molecular signatures associated with disease progression.

Cancer Biostatistics

Applying survival analysis, Bayesian methods, and predictive modeling to cancer outcomes and precision medicine.

Bayesian Methods

Developing and applying Bayesian approaches for clinical trials, biomedical research, and complex longitudinal data.
